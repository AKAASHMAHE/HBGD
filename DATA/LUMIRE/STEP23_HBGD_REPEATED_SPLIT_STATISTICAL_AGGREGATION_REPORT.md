## Interpretation

The repeated-split analysis demonstrates a directionally favorable and reasonably consistent incremental effect of transferred HBGD information.

The split-level bootstrap confidence interval crosses zero.

The exact sign test does not establish that the proportion of positive splits differs from chance at the 0.05 level.

The best-split sensitivity analysis evaluates whether the average effect is dependent on the single most favorable split. The worst-split sensitivity analysis evaluates the influence of the most unfavorable split.

## Scientific conclusion

Repeated patient-level splitting provides additional robustness evidence for the cross-cancer HBGD transfer hypothesis because the transferred representation was evaluated across multiple independently constructed target-domain cohorts. Nevertheless, the effect size is modest and split-dependent. Statistical inference from only ten repeated splits should be considered exploratory rather than definitive.

The appropriate overall interpretation is therefore that transferred HBGD information shows preliminary and directionally reproducible complementary predictive information beyond target-domain geometry, but cross-cancer transferability has not yet been statistically or clinically established.
